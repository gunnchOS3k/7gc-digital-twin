# Paper I novelty boundaries

| Closest prior method | Established capability | What this package adds | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sterbenz et al., resilience framework (2010) | Service acceptability under challenges, multilevel operational/service states | Small executable state/workload corpus and evidence audit | First service-centered resilience framework |
| Satyanarayanan, edge computing (2017) | Nearby processing and fallback during cloud outage | Explicit stored support/failure rows | New offline-fallback architecture or measured edge benefit |
| EdgeDroid (2019) | Trace replay, emulated human-in-the-loop tasks and measured client/cloud timing | Reproducible synthetic semantics and failure-coverage critique | Comparable application execution, trace realism or benchmark superiority |
| Fuller et al., digital-twin survey (2020) | Twin definitions, enabling technology and data challenges | Clearly bounded artifact provenance | Sensor-synchronized physical twin |
| Li et al., native network twin (2025) | Real-time data, network-twin architecture and reported real-network comparison | Auditable implementation-specific seed/proxy characterization | Equivalent network validation or independent novelty priority |

The mathematical integrations and four-state classifier are elementary. Reproducibility can be valuable without constituting a sufficient original journal contribution. Closest methodological competition is trace/workload benchmarking (EdgeDroid), while the service-state idea predates this work (Sterbenz). The inspected bibliography is not an exhaustive originality search. Full-text review is incomplete for Fuller and Li; claims have been restricted to inspected full-text sections, available abstracts/metadata and contextual statements. Human originality review and journal significance remain pending. No competitor result is reproduced or numerically compared. No full novelty claim is marked verified.
